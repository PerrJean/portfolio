## The job in three sentences

I am Head of Product at an online language-learning platform, a role much like a film director’s. I’m not the one behind the camera: the developers build the product and the designers draw the screens. With them, I decide what we build for learners; like the producer, the leadership team has the final say on the big decisions.

## I lead my team

My team has one Product Manager and two Product Designers. I meet each of them one-to-one every week. I help them grow and I assess their performance. With the Product Manager, I reviewed a [skills matrix](/en/projects/skills-matrix/) so that we agree on what “becoming senior” means.

When I hired a Product Designer, I screened the applications and told HR which candidates to call first. I ran two interviews, the second with a case study. I then presented two candidates to the Chief Product Officer (CPO), my manager, and to the CEO, and explained which one I preferred and why.

I give them whole projects to run, and I teach them to work with the developers as the learners’ voice: start from what learners say they need, and keep coming back to it. On their discovery projects, I decide with them what is worth producing, and we sign it off together.

## I work with the other teams

With the Chief Technology Officer (CTO) and the two lead developers, I adjust how we work, meetings included. Together, we decide what to work on next.

I take every request to the developers, and together we weigh what a change costs against what it brings.

With the learning designers, who create the teaching content, I start from their needs and together we build the tools they lack. The content audit, for example, checks every exercise question.

The account managers look after our client companies and bring me their problems and needs. In return, I give them tools to renew contracts and sell more.

With the CEO, I prepare the product committee and its presentation. It is the meeting where the leadership team approves major decisions.

## Three strengths

**Listening before building.** Before we developed the [client dashboard](/en/projects/client-dashboard/), I brought five clients together in a workshop, so that we could start from the decisions they have to make. Live since June, it has been adopted: more than half of the organisations that opened it in September came back in October.

**Leading a team through to results.** In the [speaking role-plays](/en/projects/speaking-practice/), an AI plays the other person in the conversation. I set out the method, then the Product Manager and the Product Designer interviewed clients. The project brought in several tens of thousands of euros in additional sales to existing client companies.

**Putting AI to work for quality.** In the [content audit](/en/projects/content-audit/), a learning designer and I had an AI check every exercise question in an initial course. One in 20 kept learners from getting the answer right, and all of them have been fixed.
