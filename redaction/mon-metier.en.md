## The job in three sentences

I’m Head of Product at an online language-learning platform, a role much like a film director’s. I’m not the one behind the camera: the developers build the product and the designers design the screens. With them, I decide what we build for learners; as with a film producer, the leadership team has the final say on major decisions.

## I lead my team

My team has one Product Manager and two Product Designers. I have a one-on-one with each of them every week. I help them grow and I assess their performance. With the Product Manager, I reviewed a [skills matrix](/en/projects/skills-matrix/) so that we would mean the same thing by “becoming senior”.

When I hired a Product Designer, I screened the applications and told HR which candidates to call first. I ran two interviews, the second with a case study. I then presented two candidates to the CEO and to my manager, the Chief Product Officer (CPO), and explained which one I preferred and why.

I give them whole projects to run. I also teach them to speak up for learners when working with the developers: start from what learners say they need, and keep coming back to it. On their discovery projects, I decide with them what is worth producing, and we approve it together.

## I work with the other teams

With the Chief Technology Officer (CTO) and the two lead developers, I adjust the way we work, including how we run meetings. Together, we decide what to work on next.

I take every request to the developers, and we weigh the cost of each change against its benefits.

With the learning designers, who create the teaching content, I start with what they need, and we build the tools they are missing together. One of them, the content audit, checks every exercise question.

The account managers look after our client companies and bring me their problems and needs. In return, I give them tools to renew contracts and sell more.

With the CEO, I prepare the product committee meeting, where the leadership team approves major decisions, and the presentation for it.

## Three strengths

**Listening before building.** Before we developed the [client dashboard](/en/projects/client-dashboard/), I brought five clients together in a workshop, so the dashboard would be built around the decisions they have to make. It went live in June, and clients use it: more than half of the organisations that opened it in September came back in October.

**Leading a team through to results.** In the [speaking role-plays](/en/projects/speaking-practice/), an AI takes the part of the person the learner is talking to. I set out the method, then the Product Manager and a Product Designer interviewed clients. The project brought in several tens of thousands of euros in additional sales to existing client companies.

**Using AI to improve content quality.** In the [content audit](/en/projects/content-audit/), a learning designer and I started with one course and had an AI check every exercise question in it. One question in 20 made learners fail through no fault of their own. All of them are now fixed.
